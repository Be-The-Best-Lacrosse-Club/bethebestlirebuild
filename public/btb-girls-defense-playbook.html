Defense = Feet + Position + Voice

In the women's game you cannot body-check your way out of trouble. Your tools are your feet, your stick position, your reads, and your voice. A silent defense is a beaten defense. Every concept in this playbook depends on loud, specific talk between all seven defenders, goalkeeper included.

Two rules shape everything we do: shooting space and the 3-second rule. They mean you cannot pack the 8-meter and wait. Every defender has to be active, legal, and connected on every possession.

BTB Girls Defensive Communication System

One word per job. Every BTB defender uses these exact calls, every possession, loud enough to hear over wind and crowd. Every tab in this playbook uses this same vocabulary.

The Calls

"I'VE GOT BALL!"I am on the ball carrier. Say it every time the ball moves to your player.
"HELP!"I am one pass away and I am the first slide. I can see my player and the ball, and I am ready to go.
"GO!"The on-ball defender is beaten and I am sliding now. When you hear it, the whole defense rotates.
"SECOND!"I have the player the first slider left. The second rotation.
"RECOVER!"The crisis is over. Sprint to a player. Every recovery ends within a stick's length of an attacker.
"PICK LEFT!" / "PICK RIGHT!"A screen is being set on my teammate. Called by the screener's defender the moment the picker starts moving.
"OVER!" / "UNDER!" / "SWITCH!"Our answer to the pick. Both defenders say it back. A one-sided switch is an open shot.
"CUTTER!"An off-ball attacker is cutting into the 8-meter. Nearest defender steps into her path.
"MARK UP!"Everyone find a body. Used after scrambles, turnovers, and the draw.
"BALL TOP / LEFT / RIGHT / LOW!"Ball location calls, owned by the goalkeeper. They shift the whole unit, especially when we are down a player.
"CLOCK!"Under 30 seconds on the shot clock. No reaching, no gambling. Make them shoot over us.

Key Terms

Women's Lacrosse Defensive Terminology

8-Meter ArcThe scoring zone in front of the goal. Most goals come from here. You must be actively marking to stand inside it.
12-Meter FanThe wider arc outside the 8. Outer edge of the critical scoring area.
Critical Scoring AreaThe high-danger zone in front of the goal. Every slide and rotation is built to keep the ball out of it.
Adjacent SlideThe defender closest to the ball carrier leaves her player to stop the drive. Everyone else rotates behind her.
BackerA defender who plays behind the on-ball pressure inside the 8, reading the play instead of marking one attacker. She is the safety net if the first slide fails. Because of the 3-second rule she drifts on and off the arc rather than camping in it.
Open StanceBody angled so you see your player and the ball at the same time. Never turn your back on either.
90-Second Shot ClockThe offense must shoot within 90 seconds of possession. Patient defense turns the clock into a weapon.

Shooting Space

You cannot stand in the lane between a shooter and the goal unless you are within a stick's length of an attacker and actively marking her. Slide to the player, not to the space in front of the goal. Lead with your stick, approach at an angle, and arrive within a stick's length. A violation is a free position for the offense, and it is the most common foul in the women's game.

3-Second Rule

No defender may stay inside the 8-meter arc for more than three seconds unless she is within a stick's length of an attacker. If nobody is in your zone, get out of the 8 and reposition on the edge. The referee counts silently, and at three the whistle blows and it is a free position. Help defenders live on the edge of the arc: one foot in, one foot out, stepping in to bump and stepping back out.

Coach's Note: The Defensive Mindset

Great defenders in the women's game share three habits. They talk constantly. They move their feet instead of reaching with their stick. And they play with discipline, never gambling on a check unless they are certain. A missed check is a foul and a reach into shooting space is a free position. Stopping the ball with body position beats any stick check.

Slides & Recovery

The slide is the most important team concept in women's defense. When a ball carrier beats her defender, the adjacent defender leaves her player to stop the ball and every other defender rotates to fill. It is a chain reaction. One move triggers the next until the defense is reset.

The Slide Rotation

  1. On-Ball Defender: Gets beaten. She does not quit. She recovers inside first and finds the player the slide left open.
  2. First Slide (Adjacent): Has already called "HELP." When the dodger's hips get past her teammate's hips, she calls "GO" and sprints to the ball. Her job is to stop the ball, not to check it.
  3. Second Slide: Calls "SECOND" and takes the player the first slider left.
  4. Backer / Low Defenders: Shift toward the crease to protect the most dangerous area and pick up any cutter.
  5. Goalkeeper: Runs the whole rotation. She sees everything, calls who is open, and calls "RECOVER" when the ball moves out.

Coach's Note: Recover to a Player

Recovery is where youth defenses break down. The slide is good, the ball moves, and nobody sprints back. In the women's game recovery is even more important because you cannot recover to a spot inside the 8. You recover to a player, within a stick's length, or you get called for three seconds. In practice, after every slide drill blow the whistle and make every defender point to the attacker she is marking.

Slide Timing

Slide when the dodger's hips pass your teammate's hips. Earlier and the attacker pulls back and hits the player you left. Later and she is already in shooting position. Read hips, not the stick.

Practice Drill: 3v2 Slide and Recover

Three attackers around the 8-meter, two defenders plus a goalie. Ball starts at the top. The ball carrier drives, one defender slides, the third attacker is open. Can the beaten defender recover and close out before a clean shot? Full speed, every rep. Getting beaten is not the end of the play. The recovery is the play.

Defending Picks

You will see off-ball picks every game. You cannot fight through a screen with your body, so you need technique, an early call, and a plan. We carry four answers.

Four Ways to Defend a Pick

  1. Over: Work over the top of the pick and stay attached to your player. Default against a strong shooter. You have to anticipate the pick to get your body past the screener's hip in time. Call "OVER."
  2. Under: Go between the screener and the goal. Takes away the drive, gives up a longer shot. Use it against a driver who is not a shooter. Call "UNDER."
  3. Switch: Swap players. Fastest fix when the pick is set too well to fight, but it can create a mismatch. Call "SWITCH" and hear "SWITCH" back.
  4. Hedge: The screener's defender steps out for one beat to slow the cutter, then snaps back to the screener before she rolls. Advanced. Takes trust and timing. Call "PICK" first, then hedge.

Coach's Note: The Screener's Defender Talks First

The most important player in pick defense is not the one being screened. It is the defender guarding the screener, because she can see the pick coming. She calls "PICK LEFT" or "PICK RIGHT" early enough for her teammate to prepare. In practice, if the pick is not called, stop the drill. No call means the offense wins for free.

Practice Drill: 2v2 Pick Defense

A screener and a cutter against two defenders. The coach names Over, Under, or Switch before each rep until all three are automatic. Then remove the call and let the two defenders decide together, out loud, in real time. If both defenders pick the same option without talking, it does not count.

Off-Ball Defense & Help

While everyone watches the ball, an attacker makes a V-cut, slips behind her defender, and flashes into the 8 for a feed and finish. Defending it takes an open stance, weak-side awareness, bump responsibility, and legal footwork inside the arc.

Ball-You-Player

Every off-ball defender sits in a triangle between her player, the ball, and the goal. The closer the ball is to your player, the tighter you play her. The farther away, the more you sag toward the goal.

  • Two or more passes away: Sag toward the goal. You are in full help mode and your first job is protecting the middle.
  • One pass away: Split the difference. You must be able to close out if the pass comes and slide if the driver beats her defender. The hardest spot on the field.
  • Your player has the ball: Full denial. Feet moving, stick active, body between her and the goal.

Bumping Cutters Inside the 8

  • See it early: Open stance, head on a swivel, never fully turned away from ball or player.
  • Call it: "CUTTER." The goalkeeper calls it too. She has the best view.
  • Beat her to the spot: Do not chase. Step to where she is going and get there first with your body set. No contact needed. You are occupying the space she wants.
  • Recover: After the bump, find your player or the most dangerous open attacker. Do not stand in the 8.

Coach's Note: Help Position and the 3-Second Rule

You want your help defenders near the arc to bump cutters and slide on drives, but the 3-second rule says they cannot stay inside it unmarked. The answer is the drift: live on the edge of the 8, one foot in and one foot out. Step in to bump. Step out when nobody is cutting. Practice it until it is automatic.

Practice Drill: 4v4 Cutter Defense

Two attackers with the ball up top, two off-ball near the 8. The off-ball attackers take turns cutting through. Defenders call "CUTTER," bump, and recover while keeping their triangles. A cutter who catches the ball unmarked inside the 8 is a point for the offense. A bump that changes her path is a point for the defense. First to five.

Disadvantage Defense

A yellow card sends a player off for two minutes and her team plays a player down. In our end that means six field defenders plus the goalkeeper against seven attackers. Someone is always open. Our job is to make the open player the least dangerous one, eat clock, and survive.

BTB Backer Zone (7v6)

You cannot mark seven players with six, so we drop into a zone built around the 8-meter arc with a backer inside it.

  1. Three High: Left, center, and right across the top of the 8. They pressure the ball above the arc and force it wide, never through the middle.
  2. Two Low: Either side of the crease near goal line extended. They own the low feeds and the low cutters.
  3. Backer: Floats inside the 8 behind the pressure. She takes any cutter who enters, then drifts back out to stay legal on the 3-second count.
  4. Goalkeeper: Calls "BALL TOP / LEFT / RIGHT / LOW" to move the whole unit and identifies the most dangerous attacker.

Pack the 8

Let them have the ball outside the 12. Let them pass it around. Make them earn every step toward the goal. With a 90-second shot clock, a two-minute penalty is roughly one full possession and part of another. The mindset is not "survive two minutes." It is "survive one possession."

Coach's Note: Talk When You Are Down

  • "BALL TOP!" The three high defenders pressure. The two low stay home.
  • "BALL LEFT!" / "BALL RIGHT!" The zone shifts to the ball side. Weak-side defenders collapse toward the crease.
  • "BALL LOW!" Ball behind the goal or below GLE. Everyone packs tight around the crease. This is the most dangerous look for us.
  • "CLOCK!" Under 30 seconds. No reaching, no gambling. Make them shoot over the zone.

Practice Drill: 7v6 Shot Clock

Six defenders and a goalie against seven attackers with a visible 90-second clock. The defense has one goal: no goal. Give up the perimeter passes. Contest every shot inside the 8. After each rep, note how much clock the offense used. Under 30 seconds and someone reached or gambled. Over 60 seconds and the defense did its job, goal or not. Train patience, train trust, train the zone.
