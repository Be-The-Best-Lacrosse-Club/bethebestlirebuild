The 80/20 Rule

Permission to Be Great
The Framework
80%
Play Smart
20%
Chase Greatness

Composure + Creativity

A simple, repeatable framework for balancing IQ and instinct in recruiting events. It gives players the structure to play intelligently — and the freedom to chase greatness without fear.

The Tension Every Player Feels

Two Ways to Lose

Safe Play = Invisible Play

Players think playing safe will earn a good evaluation. In reality, it leads to:

  • Blended-in performances
  • Low activity
  • Zero standout moments
  • No separation from peers
  • No reason to remember your number
No coach builds a board around the safest player.
Too Much Risk = Bad Evaluation

Players who force every play look:

  • Turnover-prone
  • Poor in decision-making
  • Uncoachable
  • Outside the team concept
  • Inconsistent or emotional
Playmaking ≠ Recklessness.

Preparing for the Spotlight

Marathon. Not Sprint.
THE COLSEY RULE

It\'s a Marathon.
Not a Sprint.

Most athletes enter their recruiting year having played years of club and high school lacrosse. But the expectations shift the moment college coaches start watching. The year between fall sophomore and summer tournaments is the most important window of your career.

The fall of your sophomore year is when coaches build their tracking list. The summer tournaments are when those lists become recruiting boards. The months between are where the real work gets done.

The Reality of Fall

Fall Is a Starting Point — Not a Verdict

The typical club program plays three fall tournaments during a player\'s sophomore year. While these matter, the fall is only the first step.

Coaches use it primarily to create an early list of players worth tracking. A strong showing helps. A weaker showing doesn\'t change much.

The Real Opportunity Begins After HS Season Ends.
The Three Pillars

What to Work On — November Through Summer

01
PILLAR
Technical Development
Focus Areas
  • Stickwork
  • Shooting
  • Positional discipline
  • Lacrosse IQ
Why It Matters Now

This is the window where the most meaningful technical improvements occur. Without a game-week grind, players can train, repeat, and refine without burning out.

02
PILLAR
Physical Development
The Window

Strength and speed training should be prioritized between November and April.

Once the spring season begins, it becomes nearly impossible to increase strength and power while competing multiple times per week.

Why It Matters Now

You build the engine in the off-season. You use the engine during recruiting season. Players who skip this step show up to summer weaker than the kid in the next jersey.

03
PILLAR
Academic Performance
The Filter

GPA is one of the first filters coaches apply. Strong grades create more opportunities and broaden the range of schools where an athlete can realistically gain admission.

Why It Matters Now

Every transcript line is recruiting capital. Sophomore and junior year grades carry the most weight. Your GPA opens doors that talent alone cannot.

The Summer Arrives

The Most Important Stage

By the Time Summer Hits

Show Up Physically Stronger. Technically Sharper. Academically Prepared.

Summer tournaments represent the most important stage of the entire recruiting process. The months leading up to that period must be used intentionally.

You don\'t out-work the field at the tournament. You out-work them in the months before.

The Players Who Win The Marathon

Don\'t arrive at summer hoping to peak. Arrive at summer having already peaked. Train through the winter, sharpen through the spring, perform through the summer.

The Prospect Day Trap.

Prospect days are direct interactions with a coaching staff that can be useful — but they are widely overused and widely misunderstood. More is not better. Intentional is better.

Most families treat an invitation like recruitment. It is not. Programs send hundreds of invitations. The number that come back with real offers is a small fraction. The number that come back with burnt-out families is much higher.

Three Lies Families Believe

What Prospect Days Are NOT

Lie 01 · "I Got Invited — I\'m Being Recruited"

An invitation is not recruitment. It is exposure marketing. Programs send hundreds of invitations to fill paid events.

If a coach wants you, they tell you. They do not bury it in an event mailer.

Lie 02 · "Showing Up = Real Interest"

Attending a prospect day does not guarantee interest. It guarantees you stood on the field. Many staffs review attendees the same way they review tournament rosters — quickly, and most names are passed.

Lie 03 · "More Events = More Exposure"

Wrong. Most prospect days are low-efficiency exposure events. Many of them generate significant income for the coaching staff — and very little recruitment return for the athlete.

Be Aware of Who is Paying Whom.
The Real Cost

Too Many Prospect Days = Burnout

The Hidden Cost

What Over-Attending Actually Buys You

  • Burnt-out player
  • Burnt-out parents
  • Thousands of dollars in fees, travel, hotels
  • Weekends gone — for very little measurable return
  • A schedule that crowds out the work that actually moves the needle
Quantity Is Not a Strategy.
The Smart Approach

How to Use Prospect Days Correctly

01
Attend just 1-2 targeted prospect days per year — only at schools that are genuine fits.
02
Combine with broader exposure events. Prospect days are not a substitute for tournaments and showcases — they are a supplement.
03
Use them to evaluate fit both ways. You are looking at the program as much as they are looking at you.
04
Be intentional — not reactive. Every invitation is not an opportunity. Most are noise.

Selective Beats Saturated

The families who treat prospect days as a checklist burn out. The families who treat them as strategic visits get results.

Save your money. Save your weekends. Save your son\'s energy for the events that matter.

Be Intentional.
Not Reactive.
Two Targeted Beats Ten Random.

The Roster Audit

How To Scout A Program Before They Scout You
The Script Flip

They\'re Not Just Recruiting You.
You\'re Recruiting Them.

Most families spend hours on highlight reels and zero hours on the program they want to play for. Wrong move. Roster research is the most overlooked — and the most powerful — way to evaluate a real fit.

A roster tells you the truth a campus visit cannot. How many midis are graduating? How stacked is attack? Are they bringing in transfers every year? Answer these questions before you spend a single email on a program.

The Three Layers

How To Audit Any Program In 30 Minutes

01
LAYER ONE
The Current Roster
What To Look For

Examine the roster by class year and position. Where is the depth? Where is the gap?

  • Seniors at your position
  • Underclassmen depth
  • Starters vs. backups
  • Position breakdown
What This Tells You

Depth + early opportunity. If they have 8 seniors at your position graduating in your freshman year, that is a fit. If they have 4 freshmen who locked it down — that is a wall.

02
LAYER TWO
The Recruiting Classes
What To Look For

Research the 2025, 2026, and 2027 classes (the players ahead of you). Who has committed? At what positions? How many players per class?

  • Commits by class year
  • Commits by position
  • Class size pattern
  • Where they recruit from
What This Tells You

Their pattern is your forecast. If they take 4 midis a year and they already have 4 committed in your class — your window is closed.

03
LAYER THREE
The Transfer Portal History
What To Look For

Pull 2-3 years of transfer portal activity. Who did they bring in? Who left? What positions?

  • Transfers in (annual count)
  • Transfers out
  • Transfer positions
  • Who lost their job
What This Tells You

Some programs aggressively bring in transfers — and those transfers take starting jobs from returning players. Knowing their patterns protects you from surprises later.

The New Reality

The 48-Man Roster Limit

The Hard Cap

A typical 48-man roster breaks down roughly like this:

Across 4 classes, that means roughly 2–3 midfielders and 1–2 attackmen recruited per class. Once those spots fill, the door closes.

The Hidden Threat

The Transfer Portal Trap

What No One Tells You

Transfers Can Steal Your Spot — Even After You Commit.

Some programs treat the transfer portal as a primary recruiting tool. They:

  • Bring in 4-6 transfers per year
  • Hand transfers immediate starting jobs
  • Push returning players down the depth chart
  • Use the portal to fix gaps your class was supposed to fill

If you commit to a heavy-portal program without knowing their pattern, you can spend two years working for a starting spot — and watch a transfer take it in your junior season.

Build The Athlete

Lift · Run · Sleep · Fuel · Recover
The Separation Game
11,000 vs. 770
Players Competing For D1 Roster Spots

Off-Field Habits Build On-Field Players.

Skill plateaus. The off-field game compounds. In a recruiting pool this crowded, your physical preparation is one of the few places you can open daylight. Strong, rested, well-fueled athletes move better, play harder, and last longer.

Pillar 1 · Lift

How Lacrosse Strength Is Different

01
PRINCIPLE
Leg Strength → Bodyweight

Leg strength relative to bodyweight is one of the biggest predictors of speed and agility on the field.

Why It Matters

You don\'t need the heaviest squat in the gym. You need the strongest legs for your size. Lacrosse rewards power-to-weight, not raw numbers.

02
PRINCIPLE
Single-Leg Dominates

Nearly every movement in lacrosse is unilateral — one leg at a time. Dodges, cuts, shots, sprints.

Why It Matters

If your training is dominated by two-legged work, you\'re training for a sport you don\'t play. Single-leg exercises mirror what the field actually demands.

03
PRINCIPLE
Functional Beats Isolation

Compound, athletic-pattern movements build usable strength. Bicep curls and machine work build nothing transferable.

Why It Matters

Functional movements are safer, more efficient, and they transfer directly to the field. Train for performance — not for the mirror.

The Five-Exercise Foundation

Hex Bar Deadlift
RFE Split Squat
Push-Ups
Pull-Ups
Core Work

Safe. Compound. A 3-day program built around these five movements transforms your game.

Pillar 2 · Run

Forget The 40-Yard Dash

40-Yard Dash

A football metric. Tests sustained acceleration over distance you almost never hit on a lacrosse field.

Wrong Sport.
10-Yard Dash

Lacrosse is short-burst. Most plays demand explosive acceleration over 5-15 yards. The first three steps win games.

This Is The Real Metric.

Train acceleration patterns. Train first-step explosion. The 40 doesn\'t exist in your sport — the 10 lives on every possession.

The Pillars Most Players Skip

It\'s Not Just The Weight Room

Three pillars matter as much as your lift — and almost everyone neglects them. The strongest kid who doesn\'t sleep, eat, or recover gets passed by the player who does.

03
PILLAR
Sleep · 8–9 Hours
Why It Matters

Sleep is when the body repairs and builds. Less than 8 hours and you\'re training without recovery — your lifts don\'t stick, your reaction time drops, and injuries climb.

The Rules
  • 8–9 hours minimum on school nights
  • Phone out of the bedroom — full stop
  • Same bedtime weeknights — protect the schedule
  • No screens 30 min before bed
04
PILLAR
Fuel · Eat For Performance
Why It Matters

You cannot train hard, recover, or grow on junk food and missed meals. Athletes eat to perform — not to be skinny, not to be huge, but to fuel the work.

The Rules
  • Real food first — protein, carbs, fruit, vegetables
  • Breakfast every day — no skipping
  • Hydrate constantly — water, not soda or energy drinks
  • Eat within 30 min after lifts and practices
05
PILLAR
Recover · Plan The Off Day
Why It Matters

The work happens in training. The growth happens in recovery. Skip recovery and you\'re paying for training you can\'t adapt to.

The Rules
  • One full rest day per week — non-negotiable
  • Stretch and roll out daily (10 min minimum)
  • Mobility work — hips, ankles, t-spine
  • Listen to the body — pain ≠ progress
The Annual Approach

Build In The Offseason · Maintain In Season

The Off-Field Calendar

Two Modes. One Year-Long Plan.

  • Offseason (Nov–April) — build strength, capacity, and habits. 3 days lifting. Quality sleep. Eat for growth.
  • In-Season (May–July) — maintain only. Don\'t try to build during tournaments. Sleep + nutrition matter more than lifts here.
  • Find a real program rooted in long-term athletic development — not random YouTube workouts.
  • Stick to it. Consistency beats intensity. The strongest player in May is the one who didn\'t skip December — and didn\'t skip sleep.

Strong Athletes Survive The Process

Weak athletes break down or blend in.

Skill gets you watched. The off-field game keeps you healthy, helps you finish hard plays, and shows up in the fourth quarter when everyone else is gassed. Lift. Run. Sleep. Fuel. Recover. Every day.

Skill Plateaus. Habits Compound.

The Highlight Film

30 Seconds To Earn The Click
The Single Most Important Asset You Own

Your Film Is Your Business Card.

Coaches won\'t fly to see you on a hunch. They watch your film first. Everything in your recruiting outreach — every email, every conversation, every tournament invite — sends them back to one place: your highlight reel.

A great film opens doors. A mediocre film closes them. A bad film disqualifies you before a coach ever sees you in person. The standards are specific — and most player films get them wrong.

The 30-Second Reality

How Coaches Actually Watch Film

The Real Attention Window
30–90
Seconds Of Watch Time · Per Player

If You Don\'t Hook Them Early, You\'re Closed.

A college coach has 200 highlight tabs open and 90 minutes between meetings. They are not watching your 8-minute opus. They are scanning the first 30 seconds and making a call.

The Standard

What A Great Film Looks Like

01
STANDARD
Length · 2 to 3 Minutes Max

Anything longer says you can\'t self-edit. 2–3 minutes is the sweet spot. If you have less great content, make it shorter. Don\'t pad.

02
STANDARD
Best Play First · Always

Lead with your strongest, most impressive play. If the first 10 seconds aren\'t great, the rest doesn\'t get watched. The film is front-loaded or it\'s nothing.

03
STANDARD
Clear Identification · Every Clip

Spotlight, arrow, or circle on you at the start of every clip. Coaches should never have to guess which player you are. Your number on the jersey doesn\'t count.

04
STANDARD
Variety · Show The Full Game

Coaches want to see range: dodges, finishes, assists, defense, ground balls, hustle plays. Don\'t just show 8 goals from inside 5 yards. Show complete player.

05
STANDARD
Quality · Hudl Or YouTube

Hudl is the lacrosse standard. YouTube as backup. Phone video, low resolution, shaky cam — instant credibility hit.

The Killer Mistakes

What Closes A Coach\'s Tab

Remove These Immediately

Highlight Reel Sins.

  • Opening montage with music drama — coaches skip past it
  • Slow motion clips before plays — wastes their time
  • Music with curse words — instant judgment on character
  • Clips longer than 8–10 seconds each — they need rhythm, not stories
  • Game-tying or "clutch moment" titles — show, don\'t tell
  • Saving best plays for the end — they won\'t make it there
  • No player identification — coaches won\'t hunt for you
Cut Anything That Doesn\'t Sell You In 5 Seconds.
The Delivery

How To Send The Film

Every Coach Email

Put The Link Above The Signature

  • Direct link in the email body — not an attachment, not "see below"
  • Hudl link first — coaches recognize and trust it
  • YouTube link as backup — some coaches block Hudl on work networks
  • One link. Not three. Don\'t make the coach pick.
The Maintenance

Refresh Quarterly

Your film is not "set it and forget it." After every major event — a strong tournament, a great HS game, a college clinic — add to it. Take out older, weaker clips. Keep the reel fresh.

A film with last spring\'s clips in February tells coaches you stopped working. A film updated three weeks ago tells them you\'re a current player worth tracking.

The Film Is The Door

You can write the best email in the world. You can have the best GPA. You can attend every right event. None of it matters if a coach clicks your film and shuts it in 8 seconds.

The reel earns the visit. The visit earns the offer. Get the reel right first.

  • Tag wisely — when posting highlights, tag your club, your school, and (sparingly) the programs recruiting you
  • Use the right hashtags — #lax2028 (your class), #LongIslandLax, recruiting-specific tags
  • Pin your best content — first thing a coach sees should be your top film clip

Your Digital Footprint Follows You

Coaches recruit the complete picture. The player on the field. The student on the transcript. The kid on the sideline. And the person on social media.

Lock down personal. Build the recruiting profile. Apply the 5-year rule to every post. Use the platforms as a tool — not a trap.
