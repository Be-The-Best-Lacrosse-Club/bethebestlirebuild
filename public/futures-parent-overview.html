What Parents Should Know

More Than A Free Clinic.

Futures is the first step into the BTB standard. The goal is simple: build confident young players who love the game and learn the right habits early.

For the player

High Energy Reps

Stickwork, footwork, ground balls, shooting, small-sided games, and coaching that keeps young players engaged.

For the parent

Clear Next Steps

Meet the directors, understand the development path, and see what joining BTB actually looks like.

For the club

Find Families Who Fit

BTB works best with families who value development, effort, communication, and a team-first standard.

Our Culture Built Us

Hard Work Is The Standard.

BTB is family-first, but it is not casual. We expect effort, coachability, communication, and pride in doing things the right way.

BTB Motto

Our Culture Built Us. Our Hard Work Made Us.

That line is not decoration. It is how we coach, how we practice, and how we talk to players after both wins and losses.

  • Players learn that effort and attention to detail matter before talent does.
  • Parents are treated as partners. Communication is part of the program, not an afterthought.
  • Coaches are expected to plan, teach, correct, and hold the standard consistently.
  • Boys and girls programs are separate, with leadership and coaching built for each side.
Where This Goes

The Futures Path.

The long-term vision is to build players from their first catch and throw all the way into their recruiting years with the same language, habits, and accountability.

01

K-2 Futures Foundation

Fun, fundamentals, confidence, athletic movement, and a first look at the BTB standard.

02

Development Teams

More consistent practices, small-sided competition, position habits, and stronger team concepts.

03

Club Travel Teams

Team training, tournament weekends, film habits, and competitive standards that prepare players for higher levels.

04

Recruiting Years

Older teams focus on film, communication, tournament placement, and preparing players for the environments where college coaches evaluate.

Practice + Winter Plan

Training Is A Year-Round Build.

Final team calendars are sent after roster placement, but the model is consistent: practices are planned, coaches teach from a curriculum, and winter is used to make players better before spring and summer.

Team practices

Structured Sessions

Written plans, timed segments, skill blocks, small-sided play, and team concepts. Players should know what they are improving every practice.

Winter window

December-February

Indoor skills, positional work, athletic movement, small-sided competition, goalie/draw work, and prep for spring.

Player habits

Reps Between Reps

Wall ball, ground balls, dodging footwork, and simple film habits. The best players do more than show up.

Team-specific dates, venues, and winter blocks are confirmed by age group after rosters and facility windows are locked.

Competition + Recruiting Prep

We Do Not Chase Random Weekends.

Tournament schedules depend on age and team level. The standard is focused competition, not overloading families with events that do not help players develop.

Tournament volume

Focused Calendar

Younger teams build through local competition and age-appropriate events. Older club teams typically play a focused set of summer and fall tournaments, with volume adjusted by age, level, and recruiting needs.

Why top events matter

Prepare Before The Spotlight

As players get older, BTB looks for events that test pace, decision-making, physicality, and composure. The goal is to make recruiting-year tournaments feel earned, not overwhelming.

  • We place teams where the competition level fits their development stage.
  • We use tournament weekends to teach preparation, sideline behavior, film habits, and response to adversity.
  • Recruiting preparation starts before high school through skill habits, confidence, and learning how to compete.
