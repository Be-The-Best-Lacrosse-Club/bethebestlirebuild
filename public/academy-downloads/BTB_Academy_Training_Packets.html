01

Ground Ball Drill Sheet

Ground balls are not random. They reward body position, commitment, communication, and the first clean exit after possession.

Technique
  • Approach through the ball, not to the ball.
  • Butt low, chest over stick, top hand near plastic.
  • Scoop through and accelerate for three steps.
  • Protect first, then move the ball.
Daily Reps
  • 20 stationary scoops each hand.
  • 20 rolling scoops right side.
  • 20 rolling scoops left side.
  • 10 scoop, roll away, pass reps.
Coach Standard
  • Call "ball" early.
  • Do not reach from behind.
  • Win the ball with your feet.
  • Make the first pass simple.
BTB cue: ground balls are culture reps. The player who commits first usually wins.

03

BTB Footwork Ladder Circuit

Footwork should connect directly to lacrosse movement: approach, recover, dodge, rotate, and re-accelerate.

Block A

  • Two feet in each box.
  • Ickey shuffle.
  • Lateral in-in-out-out.

Block B

  • Approach and drop step.
  • Split dodge foot switch.
  • Roll dodge pivot and escape.

Block C

  • Five-yard burst after every pattern.
  • Catch and throw after footwork.
  • Finish with a ground ball.
BTB cue: fast feet only matter when they help you make the next lacrosse play.
04

Mental Performance Journal

Use this after practices, games, and Academy lessons. The goal is honest reflection that leads to better next actions.

After Practice

  • What did I do with full effort today?
  • Where did I lose focus?
  • What coaching point do I need to apply next?
  • Who did I make better today?

After Games

  • What was my best response to adversity?
  • What mistake do I need to own?
  • What pattern did I see on film or in the game?
  • What is my next rep target?
05

Pressure Performance Protocol

Pressure does not disappear. Players need a repeatable reset they can use during games.

Reset

  1. Exhale once.
  2. Touch stick or glove.
  3. Name the next job.

Refocus

  1. Find ball.
  2. Find matchup.
  3. Communicate one clear call.

Respond

  1. Sprint to the next spot.
  2. Win the next small play.
  3. Do not carry the last mistake.
BTB cue: the next play is the only one you can still affect.
06

Recruiting Preparation Guide

For older players: recruiting starts with fit, grades, honest evaluation, and clear communication.

Profile

  • Grad year, position, club team.
  • GPA and academic interests.
  • Height, dominant hand, key strengths.
  • Coach contact information.

School List

  • Academic fit.
  • Roster fit.
  • Location and campus size.
  • Realistic playing opportunity.

Outreach

  • Short email.
  • Clear subject line.
  • Highlight film link.
  • Upcoming tournament schedule.

08

Highlight Film Checklist

A strong film is short, organized, and honest. Show the plays that translate to the next level.

Must Include

  • Name, grad year, position, jersey number.
  • Best plays in the first 30 seconds.
  • Off-ball plays, not only goals.
  • Ground balls, defense, rides, clears, and decisions.
  • Easy-to-see player marker before each clip.

Avoid

  • Long intro graphics.
  • Slow-motion only clips.
  • Music that distracts from the play.
  • Ten clips of the same exact skill.
  • Film longer than coaches will watch.
BTB cue: highlight film should prove how you help a team win, not just that you can score.
